# Brand Strategy — Connor Willis Racing

Version 2.0 · 2026-10-16 · Built from CREATIVE_BRIEF.md v2.0

## Executive summary
Driver sponsorship is normally sold through private decks and negotiated prices, so it feels risky to the businesses who could afford it. Connor Willis Racing already owns the opposite position with this page: twenty priced surfaces on a car you can spin, a public goal and progress bar, sourced results, and two partners already on the bodywork. The strategy is to protect that clarity, add proof where it is thin, and keep the voice of a race engineer rather than a salesperson.

## Positioning statement
For business owners who want their brand on a real racing car without a negotiation, Connor Willis Racing is the sponsorship you can price, place and picture from one page, because every spot has a published price, a place on the 3D car, and a driver whose results are on the official GB4 table.

## Target audience
- Primary: US and UK company owners and marketing leads with $12,000 to $150,000 to place, often with a Chicago-area or motorsport connection. They buy on clarity, proof and a simple next step.
- Secondary: Smaller businesses and individuals for the driver kit and small surfaces; motorsport suppliers.
- Not for: Buyers who need to pay online or who want a bespoke title deal larger than the car.

## Competitive analysis
Public GB4 sponsorship pricing could not be found in research on 2026-10-16. The series publishes distribution facts (every race streamed on YouTube, Facebook and gb-4.net, TV-style production) but no audience figures, so the page must not quote any.

| Competitor type | What they do well | Gap to exploit |
|---|---|---|
| Junior single-seater driver pages and decks | Story, podium photos | Prices hidden, PDF not web, no placement visual |
| Tiered driver and team pages (£500 club tiers to 250k single-seater packs) | Clear ladder, placements listed | No proof, no reporting, desktop-only |
| "Buy a spot" pages outside motorsport (brandmymac.com) | One object, numbered spots, progress bar | Thin on trust; this page adds sourced results and real partners |
| Sponsorship agencies' small-business guides | Address the ROI objection | Vendor-generic, no offer behind the advice |
| Team pages (Hillspeed) | Authority, history | Sell the team, not a specific placement |

## Copy patterns observed (feeds /copywriter)
Strong pages lead with a number, name the deliverable, use one verb per action, place proof next to the claim, and say what happens after you click. The live page already does all five. Weak pages use "partnership opportunity", "exposure" without a number, and "Learn more" buttons. None of that is on the page; keep it that way.

## Brand personality, values, differentiators
- Personality: Straight, confident, specific, hard-working, ambitious.
- Values: Say the price. Show the place. Cite the result.
- Differentiators: Published per-spot pricing; the 3D placement view; sourced results (first win at Silverstone, podium at Brands Hatch, 9th with 221 points, Hillspeed runner-up); two founding partners; a US driver on the UK ladder, which gives US sponsors a story and UK sponsors a reach they don't usually get.

## Recommended visual directions (feeds /visual-designer)
1. Keep: "Showroom" (the live system). White ground, livery red, Barlow Semi Condensed display, 14px radii, soft shadows. It reads as credible and lets the red car carry the colour. Recommended.
2. Alternative comp: "Pit wall" (the Relume build in this project). Black ground, monza red, Racing Sans One, pinned video hero. Stronger atmosphere, weaker legibility for a dense price table, and it would need the real spot model rebuilt into it. Hold as an option for a campaign landing page, not for the sponsorship menu.
3. Reject: gradients, glass, neon, any direction that makes the table harder to read.

## Proof to add when available
- Spots marked taken with partner logos on the car (the strongest proof the page can show).
- A sentence on where the livestream can be watched, with the MSV TV link already in the spec card.
- The first partner quote, if a partner offers one. Never invent one.
